# TASK-953 French lane: bench + convert (whisper-large-v3-french)

**Date:** 2026-11-03 · **Scope:** the French community-catalog lane (GH #181, TASK-953), mirroring the Finnish lane (TASK-769, `2026-11-03-fi-bench.md`): reuse the proven whisper conversion pipeline on `bofenghuang/whisper-large-v3-french`, bench it on the same 40-clip FLEURS protocol as the quality matrix, and give a GO/NO-GO against Turbo. Nothing was committed, pushed, published, or cataloged; the HF upload, catalog entry, and device pass are the gated remainder awaiting maintainer review of these numbers.

## TL;DR

The conversion works end to end on the desktop and the candidate WINS the bench, but by a smaller margin than the Finnish lane: **7.10 WER vs Turbo's 8.59** on the same 40-clip fr_fr protocol (**17.3% relative**, against the fi lane's 21.7%), at 1.71x Turbo's download size and a decode time measured at RTF 2.415 on a loaded machine (upper bound; see the RTF caveat). Recommendation: **qualified GO, below the fi bar**: the quality win is real and every conversion gate passed (including a stronger agree-check than fi had), but 17.3% relative does not clear the ~20% fi precedent, and the cost side is identical to the fi entry's heavy footprint (1.78GB, the device leg found a 705MB RAM load delta there). The maintainer decides whether the margin justifies the lane; a lighter candidate WAS probed the same night and settles the question: the distil tier `whisper-large-v3-french-distil-dec16` beats this heavy tier on every axis (WER 6.54, 1.35GB, 2.6x faster), superseding this report's GO; see `2026-11-03-fr-distil-bench.md`. Everything below was run and read this session except where a row is explicitly labeled with its source.

## Conversion record

**Source:** `bofenghuang/whisper-large-v3-french` (MIT, sha 90a7cd1f). Full whisper-large-v3 architecture (32+32 layers, 128 mels, d_model 1280), HF safetensors 3.22GB fp32 (sha256 aa19d9e4346a332e1af644a972654326b75811bcf3226e7528aa636fc8ce30af). The repo also ships the author's own openai-format `original_model.pt` (3.09GB, sha256 0b6cec457d94c03789deae2c9b1c64ed0bb095dcab21cb45c1e152ce577b1c64), used as an independent agree-check reference.

**Toolchain (era-matched, unchanged from fi):** torch 2.3.1, onnx 1.16.2, onnxruntime 1.18.1, numpy 1.26.4, openai-whisper 20250625 (`disable_sdpa`), transformers 4.44.2; export via a patched copy of sherpa's `export-onnx-with-attention.py` at the pinned srclib commit af2d8d81 (v2.0.1), in `/var/tmp/fr-lane/export-scripts` (the fi worktree itself was not mutated). Export shape identical to fi: -attn decoder (4 outputs incl. `cross_attention_weights`, `n_alignment_heads` metadata), per-channel int8 on the decoder, 128-mel branch.

**Tokenizer verification (the fi landmine, re-checked for this checkpoint):** `vocab.json`, `added_tokens.json`, and `merges.txt` are BYTE-IDENTICAL to the Finnish repo's files (which the fi lane verified byte-identical to stock `openai/whisper-large-v3`). The same `<|yue|>`-at-50358 layout applies, so the same fix applies unchanged: `token_embedding` rows permuted into openai id order, dropping the unused `<|yue|>` row (n_vocab 51866 to 51865); the checkpoint's `proj_out` verified exactly tied to the embedding (max abs diff 0.0), so the permutation covers the output projection too. No adaptation was needed.

**Conversion sanity (run and read this session):** tied-projection diff 0.000e+00; encoder positional embedding std 0.6758 (the sinusoid), decoder positional embedding std 0.0078 (the learned turbo-class table; copied verbatim from the checkpoint, same safety argument as fi); dims `n_mels 128, n_audio_ctx 1500, 32+32 layers, n_vocab 51865`.

**Agree-check (stronger than fi's):** three decoders on two FLEURS fr probes, greedy. Our permuted `large-fr.pt` matches the author's own `original_model.pt` VERBATIM on both probes (the strongest available confirmation that our id-order convention equals the author's); the HF transformers pipeline agrees verbatim on probe0 and differs on probe1 only in two punctuation marks around "USOC"/"États-Unis" (a pipeline decode-path difference, not a weight mismatch).

**Validation (all run and read this session):**
- sherpa int8 decode of the probes matches the fp32/pt decodes; `OfflineRecognizer.from_whisper` in eval/.venv, app decode shape (tail_paddings 1000).
- Tiled 60s probe (5 clips concatenated): the 30s window decodes 71 words across 4 sentences; no premature EOT (the per-tensor-int8 failure mode).
- fp32 vs int8 word divergence: 0.00% (19-word probe) and 1.39% (71-word tiled window; one comma-word), better than the fi lane's 4.35%/6.67%.
- Timestamps (the -attn contract): 34/34 tokens carry real DTW timestamps on the probe, monotonically increasing; encoder metadata `version 2`, `n_mels 128`, `model_type whisper-large-fr`, decoder has the 4 outputs including `cross_attention_weights`.
- Known deviation (inherited from fi): `n_alignment_heads` is 320 (openai-whisper's default last-half-of-layers set) instead of the curated 10-head list in the checkpoint's `generation_config.json`, because the hand-built .pt cannot carry the official checkpoint's alignment dump; this affects timestamp head count only, not WER.

**Toolchain deviations from the fi lane:** none in the pipeline; the only differences are operational. (1) The export ran from a copied-and-patched `scripts/whisper` dir in the fr workspace rather than the fi worktree (the fi worktree stayed untouched). (2) The fi `validate_sherpa.py` timestamps leg used an API that does not exist in sherpa-onnx 2.0.1 (`OfflineRecognizer.enable_token_timestamps`); in this lane `enable_token_timestamps=True` was passed to `from_whisper` and the timestamps read off `result.timestamps` (floats, one per token). (3) The agree-check gained a third leg (the author's own `original_model.pt`) that the fi lane did not have.

## GO/NO-GO

**Qualified GO, below the fi bar; the margin call belongs to the maintainer.** The quality case: 7.10 beats every measured incumbent including Turbo's 8.59, the license is MIT, the conversion lane is the proven whisper one with a stronger agree-check than fi had (verbatim match against the checkpoint author's own openai-format export), and the int8 divergence is tighter than fi's. What weakens it relative to fi: Turbo is a stronger incumbent on fr (8.59) than it was on fi (9.05), so the relative win shrinks to 17.3%, under the ~20% fi precedent; and the cost side is unchanged from the fi entry's honest "quality option, heavy footprint" profile (1.78GB payload; the fi device leg measured a 705MB RAM load delta and RTF ~3.1 CPU on the RMX3853 for this same architecture). If the 17.3% margin is judged insufficient, the lighter alternative has since been measured: the distil tier of this same model family (`2026-11-03-fr-distil-bench.md`) scores 6.54 at 1.35GB and supersedes this heavy tier as the candidate; this report stands as the heavy tier's evidence record. Nothing ships from this report alone: the gated remainder below is where the decision lands.
